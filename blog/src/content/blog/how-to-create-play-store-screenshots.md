## What makes a good Play Store screenshot?

A strong Play Store screenshot answers three questions in seconds: **what** the app does, **why** it matters, and **whether** it looks trustworthy. That means one clear idea per image, readable headline text, and a consistent visual system—not six unrelated designs.

Google Play can surface screenshots outside your listing page (e.g., browse/search contexts). Design for **small previews first**; the full gallery is the reward for users who tap through.

## Step 1: Capture clean app screens

- Turn off **debug** banners and dev-only chrome.  
- Seed **realistic** content—names, avatars, balances—masked where needed.  
- Capture at **native resolution** from emulator or hardware; avoid upscaling tiny sources.  
- Plan **localization**: leave space for longer strings if you ship multiple languages.  

## Step 2: Choose a frame style

Match frame to audience expectation: punch-hole, notch, foldable, or clean borderless—all signal “current Android.” Stay **consistent** across the set. For ideas, browse [device frames](/frames/) and [Android app screenshot editor](/android-app-screenshot-editor/).

## Step 3: Pick screenshot story order

A practical arc:

1. **Outcome** — the promise in one headline + hero UI  
2. **Core loop** — the daily action users will repeat  
3. **Differentiator** — sync, privacy, offline, speed—pick what is true  
4. **Trust** — security, backups, enterprise controls if relevant  
5. **Delight** — widgets, wearables, themes—only if shipped  

Align with [how to make Play Store screenshots that get downloads](/blog/how-to-make-play-store-screenshots-that-get-downloads/).

## Step 4: Add headline text

Short, specific, honest copy wins. See [how to add text to app screenshots](/blog/how-to-add-text-to-app-screenshots/) for placement, fonts, and thumbnail tests.

## Step 5: Add a background

Solid, soft gradient, or structured pattern—whatever matches brand—as long as **contrast** keeps frame + type legible. Ideas: [best background ideas for app screenshots](/blog/best-background-ideas-for-app-screenshots/).

## Step 6: Export correct size

Export per **track** (phone/tablet) per Console rules—never “one JPEG for everything.” Use the [size guide](/blog/play-store-screenshot-size-guide/) and [Google Play screenshot requirements](/blog/google-play-screenshot-requirements/).

## Step 7: Upload to Play Console

- Upload into the correct **device type** slots.  
- Preview **cropping** in Console before rollout.  
- Keep filenames organized (`phone-01-en.png`) for repeat releases.  

## Example: one raw screenshot → final Play Store mockup

1. **Raw:** Emulator grab, default theme, status bar clutter.  
2. **Crop:** Hide personal data; align to key UI story.  
3. **Frame:** Android phone shell consistent with the rest of the set.  
4. **Type:** One headline (“Track habits daily”) + optional subhead.  
5. **Background:** Brand gradient with safe margins.  
6. **Export:** Phone portrait track at required resolution.  
7. **Validate:** Thumbnail legibility + policy honesty pass.

## Final checklist

- One primary message per screenshot  
- Consistent frame, typography, spacing  
- Legible text at **smallest** preview size  
- Honest UI—no fake metrics  
- Dimensions verified for **each** track  

## FAQ

### How many Play Store screenshots should I ship?

Enough to tell the story—often **four to eight** phone shots; more if each adds new information. Avoid duplicates. Dedicated note: [how many screenshots should you add to Google Play](/blog/how-many-screenshots-should-you-add-to-google-play/).

### Do I need tablet shots?

If you claim a tablet experience, show it. Otherwise stretched phone comps erode trust.
